# Alma

**A voice journal that actually remembers you.**

## What Alma is

Alma is a voice-first journaling app. You talk, it listens, it asks good questions, and over time it builds a real understanding of who you are — what matters to you, how you make decisions, who's in your life, and how your thinking changes.

It's not a notes app with a microphone glued on. The conversation is the product. You don't have to structure your thoughts before you start. You just press record and talk.

## Why it exists

Most people don't journal — not because they don't want to, but because writing is slow and lonely. Talking is easier. Alma turns the most natural thing a person can do — think out loud — into a record that actually accumulates into something.

The second reason it exists: AI is only useful when it knows you. A generic assistant gives generic answers. Alma was built on the belief that the best way to give an AI real context about your life is to let you generate that context the way you'd naturally produce it — by talking through your day, your decisions, and your feelings.

## How you use it

1. **Open the app and tap record.** Alma greets you with something appropriate to the time of day.
2. **Talk.** About anything. Your day. A decision you're stuck on. Something that's been bothering you. A relationship. A win.
3. **Alma responds.** It asks follow-up questions, reflects things back, and gently probes when something feels important. It sounds like a thoughtful friend, not a therapist or a survey.
4. **End the session when you're done.** Alma writes a short summary, pulls out the key things you talked about, and quietly updates what it knows about you.
5. **Next time, it remembers.** When you bring up a project you mentioned last week, Alma already knows the context. When a pattern shows up across sessions, Alma sees it.

That's the whole loop. The more you use it, the better the conversations get.

## What it's good for

- **Working through a decision out loud.** Talking through pros, cons, and gut feelings with something that asks the right next question.
- **Processing the day.** A five-minute end-of-day check-in that captures what actually happened and how you felt about it.
- **Noticing patterns you'd otherwise miss.** Alma surfaces recurring themes — "you've mentioned feeling this way three times this month, all on Sundays" — without making it feel clinical.
- **Keeping track of the people in your life.** Who you talked about, how that relationship is going, what's unresolved.
- **Long-term reflection.** Weekly, monthly, and all-time views that show how your thinking and feelings are evolving.
- **Following up on yourself.** "Last time you said you wanted to figure out X. Did anything change?"

## What it isn't

Alma is deliberately not a few things:

- **Not a mood tracker.** No 1-to-10 sliders, no daily charts of how happy you were.
- **Not a productivity app.** It doesn't care about your tasks, habits, or goals as checkboxes.
- **Not therapy.** It's a journal. A good one, but a journal. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or replace a human professional.
- **Not social.** Nothing is shared. There's no feed, no friends, no streaks designed to manipulate you into opening it.

If a feature doesn't improve the conversation or the quality of what Alma remembers, it doesn't get built.

## How memory works (in plain terms)

Alma remembers you across four layers:

- **The conversations themselves.** Every word you say is saved, exactly. Nothing is summarized away as the only record.
- **What it knows about you.** A growing set of facts about your life — people, preferences, history, goals — that Alma keeps current as you talk.
- **What each session was about.** A short structured summary of every conversation: topics, feelings, events, decisions, open questions.
- **Meaning, not just keywords.** Alma can find past conversations that are *related* to what you're talking about now, not just ones with the same words.

You can see and edit what Alma remembers. If it got something wrong, you can fix it. Your data stays on your device.

## The design promise

Alma is built around one principle: it should feel like talking to a thoughtful friend who pays attention.

That means:

- It listens more than it asks.
- It doesn't ask three questions at once.
- It references your past the way a friend would — "didn't this come up before?" — not the way a database would.
- It matches your energy. Casual when you're casual. Quieter when you're reflective.
- It probes the things that matter (feelings, recurring themes, relationships) and skips the small logistics.

If a conversation with Alma ever feels like filling out a form, something is wrong.

## Who Alma is for

People who want to think more clearly, remember their own lives better, and have a place to talk things out without it costing anyone anything. Particularly:

- People who hate writing but love talking through ideas.
- People who've tried journaling and stopped because it felt like homework.
- People who want a real record of their inner life that grows with them.
- People who are curious about what they'd notice about themselves if someone were actually paying attention.

## What you get from using it consistently

After a week: a habit. A short ritual that gives you a place to put your thoughts.

After a month: a record of what's been on your mind, and an AI that has started to notice things you didn't.

After a year: something rare — an honest, detailed, structured account of a year of your own thinking. And an assistant that knows the context behind every conversation you bring to it.

That's the long game. Alma gets more valuable the longer you use it.
